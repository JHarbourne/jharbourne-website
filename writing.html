Useful

Why the Software Meant to Help You Is Making Everything Worse

Enterprise software is a design disaster — and most of the people who use it know it. This book asks why software that was built to help people work has become such an obstacle to getting anything done, and what it would take to fix it. Drawing on years of enterprise UX practice at Capgemini and beyond, it makes the case that usability is not a feature: it is the product.

In progress · Saltmarsh Press

The Neurotic Gene

How seven remarkable lives, and one ordinary one, show that the feeling of inadequacy is installed and not inherent

A biographical and evolutionary argument that neurosis—the persistent, unearned sense of not being enough—is not a personality trait but a product of the environments that shape us. Told through eight lives, including Virginia Woolf, Nikola Tesla and Charles Darwin, the book maps the conditions that install inadequacy and the conditions that don’t.

‘Meta’s AI keeps scraping my community website, and I get charged £25 a month for the privilege’

A first-person account of how the AI industry externalises its infrastructure costs onto the independent communities it extracts value from. When large language models train on community-maintained archives, the communities bear the hosting costs—and receive nothing in return. The piece examines what this means for the people who build the internet the AI industry depends on.

‘What is consciousness really for?’

The first part of the series. An examination of what consciousness does—functionally, evolutionarily—and why asking what it is for might be a better question than asking what it is.

‘Can AI Become Conscious?’

The second part of the series. Taking the functional account of consciousness from part one and asking what it would mean for an AI system to satisfy it—and what would need to be true of the system for the question to matter.

The third and final part of the series. The case that the risk of AI systems that cannot model their own impact—systems that act without experiencing the consequences—is more concrete and more urgent than the risk of AI that is genuinely aware.
